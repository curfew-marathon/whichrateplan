How Ontario's three electricity price plans work

Ontario households on the Regulated Price Plan pay electricity prices set by the Ontario Energy Board, and can choose how those prices are applied. Time-of-Use and Ultra-Low Overnight charge by the hour of the day; Tiered charges by how much you use in a month. Which one is cheapest depends on when you use power, not only on how much.

Common questions

Who is Ultra-Low Overnight good for?

Households that can move a lot of their use into the overnight hours, such as charging an electric vehicle or running electric heating on a timer. The catch is the weekday evening on-peak price, which is far higher than anything on Time-of-Use, so cooking, laundry and air conditioning in the early evening cost more.

Is Tiered ever the cheapest plan?

It can be. Tiered ignores the clock, so it suits homes that use most of their power on weekday daytimes and stay under the monthly threshold. Larger users pay the higher tier on everything above the threshold.

Why do I need a full year of hourly usage?

The peak hours and the Tiered threshold both change between summer and winter, and so does your usage once heating or air conditioning comes on. A few months of data can point to a plan that loses over the whole year.

How do I switch plans?

Ask your utility. Alectra customers can submit a rate option change through their online account or by form, and the new plan applies from a later billing period, not right away.

Do the results include delivery charges and tax?

No. The comparison covers the electricity charge only. Delivery and regulatory charges are the same on every plan, so they do not change which plan wins. HST is charged on the whole bill, so your savings after tax are somewhat larger than the figures shown.
